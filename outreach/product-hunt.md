# Product Hunt launch

One shot. Sends a real traffic burst and a durable link, and the listing itself ranks.
Do it after the domain move, after the directories, and after there are people who
recognise the name — not on a quiet Tuesday because the assets happened to be ready.

## Before launch day

- **New domain live**, 301s in place. A vercel.app URL in a PH listing reads as a
  side project.
- **A gallery.** Five or six real screenshots, first one carrying the whole story: the
  requirement list with statuses. No mockups, no stock photography.
- **A 30–60 second demo video** if you can. Questionnaire → requirements → action plan →
  one policy draft. No voiceover needed; captions are enough.
- **Line up the first hour.** Not vote-begging — tell people who already know you that
  it is live and ask them to look. PH penalises obvious vote rings, so no scripts, no
  incentives, no "upvote please" messages.
- **Pick the day.** Tuesday or Wednesday, live at 12:01am Pacific. Avoid launch days with
  a heavyweight already scheduled.
- **Clear the calendar.** You need to answer every comment for 24 hours. The comment
  thread is most of the value.

## Listing copy

**Name**

> SOC2Starter

**Tagline** (60 char limit — PH's most important field)

> SOC 2 readiness for teams without a compliance department

*Alternatives:* `Know exactly what your SOC 2 audit needs, and what's missing` ·
`SOC 2 readiness and evidence tracking for 5-50 person teams`

**Description** (260 chars)

> Nineteen questions produce 29 SOC 2 requirements scoped to your actual company, a
> ranked action plan, policy drafts around your real stack, and an evidence library with
> twelve read-only connectors. Built for small teams. Prepares you for an audit — a CPA
> firm performs it.

**Topics:** Security · SaaS · Developer Tools · Productivity

**First comment — the maker comment:**

> Hi Product Hunt 👋
>
> I built SOC2Starter after watching the same thing happen repeatedly: a five-person
> startup gets told it needs SOC 2 to close an enterprise deal, opens a compliance
> platform, and is shown two hundred requirements with no indication of which ones matter
> this week.
>
> That tooling is not badly built. It is built for companies with a compliance team. Most
> companies that need SOC 2 do not have one — they have a CTO who already has a full-time
> job and a customer waiting on an answer.
>
> **So this is the opposite of a two-hour onboarding call:**
>
> 🔹 **Nineteen questions.** Plain English, five short sections, about ten minutes. A
> "no" is not a failure — it is what becomes your action plan.
> 🔹 **29 requirements, personalised.** Each carries a status derived from your own
> answers, what proves it, and who should own it.
> 🔹 **A ranked plan.** The handful of things that move your readiness most right now,

> 🔹 **Policy drafts around your real stack.** Ten templates, written for the tools you

> 🔹 **Evidence that collects itself.** Twelve read-only connectors — GitHub, Google

>
> You can leave whenever you want. Policies export as Markdown or HTML, your whole

>
> I am here all day — ask me anything, including why you might reasonably do this with a

## Launch-day discipline

- **Answer every comment**, including the critical ones, within the hour.
- **Do not ask for upvotes in the comments.** Fastest way to get a listing penalised.
- **Feed the hunters follow-up material** — reply to feature questions with a screenshot
  rather than a paragraph.
- **The link is worth more than the ranking.** Even a mediocre PH day leaves a permanent
  indexed listing. Do not treat #4 as a failure.

## After

- Add the PH badge to the site footer.
- Post the result honestly to Indie Hackers — "what launching on PH actually did for a
  solo SaaS, with numbers" is itself a linkable post, and it works whether the numbers
  are good or bad.
