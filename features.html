Getting started

Onboarding, in three screens

Your company

Name, website, headcount, industry and country. This is what makes the generated policies read like they were written for you rather than for a template.

Your stack

Pick the cloud, code host, identity provider and work tools you actually use. Your answers shape the requirements and pre-select the connectors worth adding.

Nineteen questions

Five short sections — security, access control, infrastructure, people, vendors. Plain English, no jargon. A "no" is not a failure; it is what becomes your plan.

You can run more than one company from the same account on a paid plan, and switch between them without signing out.

Dashboard

One screen that answers "how ready are we?"

A readiness estimate, scored honestly

Every requirement is scored — complete counts in full, "needs evidence" earns most of the credit because the practice exists and only the proof is missing, and anything not applicable is excluded from the denominator entirely. A requirement driven by several answers takes the worst of them, so one weak link is never hidden behind a stronger one.

Readiness over time

A chart of the estimate as it moves, so progress is visible to the person who has to report it upward.

Category scores, weakest first

Access control, infrastructure, people, policies, security and vendors — ordered so the next free hour goes where it buys the most.

Your priorities this week

The handful of items that need attention now, plus a live feed of what everyone on the team has just done.

The percentage is our readiness estimate, not an official SOC 2 score. The app says so on the same screen that shows it.

Requirements

Twenty-nine requirements, personalised — not a 200-line checklist

Each requirement arrives with a status already derived from your own answers, so you start from a real position rather than from zero.

Six categories

Policies (10), infrastructure (5), access control (4), people (3), security (3) and vendors (3) — filterable by status and category.

Five statuses

Complete, needs evidence, needs action, not started, and not applicable — the last excluded from scoring rather than quietly counted against you.

Controls underneath

Each requirement breaks into the specific controls that satisfy it, each marked satisfied or unsatisfied and linkable to the evidence that proves it.

Owners, dates and plain English

Assign an owner and a due date, read what an auditor will actually ask for, and generate a plain-English summary of why the requirement exists at all.

Each requirement also links straight to the policy that would satisfy it and the evidence hint describing what proof looks like.

Action plan

"What should we do this week?"

A ranked plan rather than a backlog: the steps that move readiness most right now, each with an owner, an effort estimate, the requirement it advances and the evidence it will produce. One tap turns any step into a task with a due date.

The trial shows the first step. The full plan is part of a paid plan.

Written around your answers

Templates fill themselves in from your company details and your stack. Optional AI generation rewrites a draft in your own context — and the templates work perfectly well without it.

Draft → in review → approved

Edit in the app, submit for approval, and have an owner or admin approve it. The requirement it satisfies flips to complete on approval.

Every version kept

Each save writes a new version with its author and timestamp. History is readable but not rewritable from the app — an auditor's favourite property.

Review dates that come back around

Each policy carries a review interval and a next-review date, and the reminder engine chases it when it comes due.

Export any policy, or the whole set, as Markdown or as HTML that opens anywhere and prints to PDF.

Evidence

A private library that knows what each file proves

Upload from anywhere

Photograph a signed document, pick a file, or attach a screenshot straight from your phone. Title, description, category and the requirement it supports.

It suggests the requirement

AI classification reads the file and proposes which requirement it proves and how long it stays valid. Without AI, keyword matching does a reasonable job.

Freshness, tracked

Every file has a review date and an expiration date, and a status of current, expiring, expired or rejected. Stale proof is a finding; the app catches it first.

Every download logged

Files live in a private bucket, downloads use short-lived signed links, and each access is written to a log the client cannot edit.

Attaching evidence to a requirement flips it to complete automatically.

Tasks and reminders

The part that keeps you ready after the audit

Tasks with real ownership

Title, description, priority, due date, an owner and additional assignees, and a status of todo, in progress, blocked or done — each linked to the requirement it advances.

Recurring compliance work

Quarterly access reviews, annual policy reviews. Complete one and the next is scheduled automatically at the interval you set.

A reminder engine

Tasks due and overdue, evidence up for review or expired, policies due for review, a task newly assigned to you, an integration that has synced, a trial about to end.

Inbox plus push

Everything lands in an in-app notification inbox with read state. Push notifications are optional — the inbox works exactly the same without them.

Security

How the product is built, since we are asking to hold your gaps

Isolation in Postgres

Every company-owned table enforces tenant isolation with row-level security keyed on company membership — not in application code, where a bug becomes a breach.

Evidence is never public

A private bucket partitioned by company, five-minute signed download links, and an access log the client can read but not write.

Credentials you cannot read back

Integration secrets live in a table with no row-level security policies at all, which means the application's own database role cannot read it under any circumstances.

History that cannot be rewritten

The activity log and policy version history have no client write path. What happened stays what happened.

Billing state is not self-grantable

Subscriptions have no client write policy. Only the signature-verified payment webhook can move billing state.

No secrets in the app

No payment, AI or provider credential is ever present in the shipped app bundle. Plan limits are enforced by database triggers, so a patched client gains nothing.

What we have not built

An honest list, so nothing is a surprise after you have paid.

  • Connectors that write. Every integration is read-only by construction. Collecting proof and changing production are different products.
  • Other frameworks. The requirement catalogue covers the SOC 2 Security criteria. ISO 27001 and HIPAA are not in it.
  • A dedicated auditor portal. The audit pack export covers the same need without giving an outsider a login.
  • Enterprise SSO.
  • Emailed invitations. An invite is created and appears for the invitee once they sign up with that address; no email is sent yet.
  • Background checks, training delivery and pen-test scheduling. SOC2Starter tracks that these happened and files the proof; it does not perform them.
